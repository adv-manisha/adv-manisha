Manisha Gandhi LLM — Business & Corporate Law

Bridging Regulation
and Practice

Manisha Gandhi is an LLM candidate in Business and Corporate Law with a keen focus on the regulatory architecture governing India's capital markets. Her academic journey is driven by a belief that rigorous legal scholarship must engage meaningfully with the practical realities of securities regulation, investor protection, and corporate accountability.

Prior to her LLM, Manisha completed her BBA LLB (Hons.), where she developed a strong foundation in contract law, company law, and administrative law. She has since honed her analytical skills through independent research and participation in national moot court competitions.

She aspires to contribute to regulatory reform dialogue, particularly around SEBI's evolving mandate, IPO disclosure norms, and the governance of listed entities in India.

Disclosure Adequacy in Indian IPOs: A Critical Review of SEBI's ICDR Regulations

Through a comparative analysis of SEBI's Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements Regulations with SEC Rule S-K and EU Prospectus Regulation, this paper evaluates whether Indian IPO prospectus norms adequately protect retail investors. Corporate Governance

Independent Directors in India: Myth, Mandate, and the Post-Uday Kotak Committee Reality

Drawing on empirical data from NSE-listed companies, this paper interrogates whether SEBI's post-Kotak Committee reforms have meaningfully enhanced independent director efficacy. It identifies structural impediments — promoter influence, information asymmetry, and liability chilling — that undermine board independence in practice.

M&A Law

Creeping Acquisitions and the SEBI Takeover Code: Plugging the Gaps in Threshold Regulation

This paper analyses how sophisticated acquirers exploit definitional ambiguities in the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011 to avoid mandatory open offer obligations. It proposes a look-through approach modelled on UK Panel practice to address regulatory arbitrage in domestic M&A transactions.
